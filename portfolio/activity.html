Normal Distribution & z-Score Explorer

Change the mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) and watch the curve move and rescale. Drag on the graph (or use the score slider) to pick a raw score X; the shaded area is the probability, and z = (X − μ) / σ. Defaults to the IQ scale (μ = 100, σ = 15).

The total area under the curve is always 1, no matter what μ and σ are - this is the distribution of scores (not a sampling distribution). Moving μ slides the whole curve left/right; changing σ stretches or squeezes its spread (the peak height adjusts so the area stays 1). The raw-score slider automatically spans μ ± 4σ, so it reaches wherever the curve does (e.g. up to 160 on the default IQ scale). Areas are computed from the standard normal, so a raw score and its z-score always cut off the same area - that is the whole point of standardizing. Values match the textbook z-table to ~4 decimals.
